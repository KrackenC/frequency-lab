TIMER

This tool plays eight families of sound, and they do not share an evidence base. Alpha and theta binaural beats have real, if modest, randomized-trial support for state anxiety and relaxation. Forty-hertz gamma stimulation is a live, unresolved research question in neurodegeneration — promising in animal models and early human trials, proven as a treatment in neither. Traditional Chinese five-element tones and Himalayan singing-bowl meditation have a genuine small clinical literature behind the practices, though not behind the specific pitches. Solfeggio tones, 432 Hz tuning, and the popular chakra-frequency set rest on twentieth-century numerology, not clinical data; any calm they produce is the calm of any pleasant tone. Pink and brown noise are the least exotic and the best-supported entries here, backed by real sleep-lab data.

Before you press play
  • This is a wellness aid, not therapy. Use it alongside — never instead of — care from a clinician for diagnosed anxiety, insomnia, or mood or neurological conditions.
  • Binaural beats only work over stereo headphones — each ear needs its own tone. Speakers will just play two audible pitches.
  • Audio-triggered seizures are rare but documented. If you have a seizure disorder, particularly one with sound sensitivity, talk to your neurologist before using rhythmic or pulsed tones.
  • Keep volume moderate and take breaks — the player caps loudness, but sustained close-headphone listening can still fatigue your ears.
  • Don't listen while driving or operating machinery, especially at the slower, drowsier delta and theta settings.
